# Eligibility: Threshold Conditions and Criteria

Sources: gov.il criteria page "תבחינים למתן רישיון להחזקת כלי ירייה פרטי" (published 21.07.2025, updated 30.06.2026) and the Firearms Regulations (Threshold Conditions and Criteria for a Private Firearm License and Additional Provisions), 5784-2023 (תקנות כלי הירייה (תנאי סף ותבחינים לקבלת רישיון פרטי לכלי ירייה והוראות נוספות), התשפ"ד-2023), as amended in 5785, 5785 (no. 2) and 5786 (the Wikisource text lists the amendments up to ק"ת תשפ"ו, 2350). Read on 28.09.2026. Where the gov.il summary and the regulation text differ, the regulation text prevails; the gov.il page itself says so.

The gov.il page states that the criteria list is closed and exhaustive: no private license or permit can be issued without meeting one of them. The regulations keep one narrow exception (regulation 11): the competent authority may grant a private license without a threshold condition or a criterion at the request of the Foreign Ministry, to secure a foreign diplomatic mission, or when the Israel Police recommended it on grounds of public peace and safety. Treat it as rare and never as a route to suggest.

## 1. Threshold conditions (tnai saf)

All of these must hold at the time of application. The regulation is section 2; the gov.il summary reads:

| # | Condition | Notes |
|---|-----------|-------|
| 1 | Registered with the Interior Ministry as a citizen or permanent resident, and resident in Israel continuously for the 3 years before the application | Exception: a person holding a teudat oleh under the Law of Return may apply under the "place of residence" criterion only, if the police approved their fitness and the other conditions hold |
| 2 | Basic command of Hebrew, enough to be questioned, understand instructions, read procedures and keep the required records | Tested in practice at the interview and the range |
| 3 | A declaration of medical fitness to carry a firearm (hatzharat briut), signed by the applicant and by a physician | The gov.il application page says "family physician". The declaration and undertaking are signed again on every renewal |
| 4 | Completion of the training program under the Firearms (Training) Regulations, 5778-2018 | See `process-stages.md` |
| 5 | A police officer under section 11(2) of the Law found no impediment on grounds of public peace and safety | The police also recommend on the residence and workplace criteria |

The same threshold requirements apply to a person requesting a permit (teudat harsha'a) to hold an organisational firearm under sections 9 to 10c of the Law.

## 2. Age and service rules

From the gov.il criteria page (section 2 of the summary):

| Situation | Minimum age |
|-----------|-------------|
| Completed regular service (sherut sadir) as defined in section 11c(b) of the Law | 18 |
| Completed 2 years of civil service (sherut ezrachi) | 21 |
| For the "place of residence" and "place of work or study" criteria: completed one year of regular service as a combat soldier, or two years of regular service | 21 |
| For the same two criteria: a woman who completed one year of regular service or one year of civil service | 21 |
| Did not complete regular service, but holds a combat certificate (teudat lochem) under IDF orders and is in a "mission chapter" (perek mesima) (regulation 2(a)(4)(a2), added 5786) | 21 (the age in section 11c(a) of the Law) |
| Israeli citizen who did not serve as above | 27 |
| Permanent resident who is not a citizen and did not serve as above | 45 |

"Regular service" in section 11c(b)(3) of the Law means service under the Defence Service Law (Consolidated Version), 5746-1986, for the period in section 15 or 16 of that law, as applicable.

## 3. Amendment 25 (2025)

Under Amendment 25 to the Firearms Law, 5785-2025 (section 11c(b)(2)), the competent authority MAY grant a license from age 18 to a person who did not complete the regular service they owed, provided that a medical committee found them unfit for service (bilti kasher le-sherut) because of their medical condition, and an officer authorised by the IDF head of personnel notified the authority of one of these:

- The person ended service because of an injury in operational activity.
- The person served as a combat soldier (lochem) under army orders and ended service for medical reasons.

The officer's notice states whether the medical unfitness also had mental-health causes, and the applicant attaches medical documents about the injury or the medical reasons. The power is discretionary ("רשאית"): meeting these conditions makes a person eligible to be considered, not entitled to a license. The gov.il criteria page summarises the route without the medical-committee condition; the Law prevails.

## 4. The 16 criteria (tavchinim)

The appendix (tosefet) to the 2023 regulations. Thresholds below are summarised from the regulation text on Wikisource, as amended through 5786, read on 28.09.2026; where the summary says "police recommendation", the recommendation is per settlement or workplace and is not published as a list, which is why the official calculator is the only reliable eligibility check.

|---|-----------|----------------|-------|
| 1 | Place of residence (makom megurim) | Centre of life in a settlement the police recommended as justifying a license | Police recommendation via the system; the calculator reflects it |
| 2 | Place of work or study | Works most of the working week, or studies at least two days a week, in a place the police recommended | Police recommendation; employer or institution affidavit (see section 5) |
| 3 | Tour guide (moreh derech) | Valid national tour-guide license from the Ministry of Tourism | The license, plus the affidavit form |

Criterion 10(a). The regulation lists an IDF officer from the rank of lieutenant (segen), an NCO from the rank of rav-samal rishon, or the rank of kam (קא"ם) or professional officer as defined in army orders, followed by the condition "אשר ביצע שירות קבע למשך חמש שנים לפחות או אשר ביצע שירות מילואים של 60 ימים לפחות במצטבר למשך תקופה של שש שנים", and their equivalents in the other security bodies, excluding professional and representative officers. The gov.il criteria tab (read 28.09.2026) lists lieutenant and rav-samal rishon and up with the same exclusion, but shows neither kam nor the 5-year and 60-day condition, and adds that requests from IDF personnel go to the IDF for checking. The regulation's sentence does not make clear whether the service condition attaches to every rank or only to the last ones. Do not resolve it for the user: quote both, and send them to the calculator and the service center.

Under criterion 2 (place of work or study), gov.il adds (section 4 of the criteria page) and regulation 6 provides: an applicant who already holds a permit from a special-license holder (an eligible settlement, plant, or government office) that is not limited to a place or time may be refused, and must choose between carrying an organisational firearm and a private license.

## 5. Affidavit forms published by gov.il

The criteria page attaches affidavit (tatzhir) forms for: salaried employee in an eligible place; self-employed in an eligible place; student in an eligible place with institution confirmation; security guard advanced course A or B; ZAKA search, rescue and recovery; ZAKA "moment of truth"; Magen David Adom; United Hatzalah; firefighter; service in the security forces; employee of a farmer; self-employed farmer; member of an agricultural cooperative; first-degree family member of a farmer; certified tour guide; YATAR (rapid response team). Send the user to download the current form from the page rather than describing its fields, because the forms change without notice.

## 6. Limits

Regulation 7(a), as amended through 5786: for criteria 1 to 12 the private license covers one handgun (ekdach) and ammunition, and no more than ONE private license is given to an applicant under criteria 1 to 12, even if several of those criteria apply (reg. 7(a)(2)). For criteria 13 to 16 the licensing official sets the type and quantity. No more than four private licenses are issued to one applicant across all criteria (reg. 7(a)(6)). A license renewed by continuity (section 9) counts toward the quota, with a carve-out for pistol licenses that had already been valid for 10 continuous years when the regulations were published (reg. 7(a)(8)).

Regulation 7(b): when a private license is issued, the licensing official sets the storage conditions. In practice this is the home-safe undertaking; see `storage-ammunition-and-incidents.md`.

## 7. When a criterion stops applying, and the holder's duty to report

The holder must report first. Regulation 3(d): when the criterion under which the license was granted stops applying, the holder shall notify the licensing official without delay ("בלא דיחוי"). Regulation 2(c) sets the same duty when the holder stops meeting threshold condition 2(a)(1) (citizenship or permanent residency with continuous residence) or 2(a)(3) (the health declaration). A holder whose health changes must therefore notify the official, not only bring an updated declaration to the next refresher. Regulation 2(a)(3) says the declaration includes the applicant's medical history, so the history belongs in it in full.

Then, from the criteria page (section 5) and regulation 3(e) to (f):

1. When the licensing official learns that the criterion under which the license was issued no longer holds, they notify the holder in writing.
2. The holder may ask to prove the criterion within 30 days of receiving the notice.
3. If not proven within 30 days, the holder must deposit the firearm and ammunition at a licensed dealer, and may prove another criterion within a period not exceeding 6 months from the first notice.
4. The total time to prove a criterion before the license is cancelled is 6 months from the first notice.

Typical triggers: moving out of a recommended settlement, leaving a recommended workplace, ending volunteer service, or a lapsed professional license.

## 8. Reservist relief for criteria 9 and 13

Regulation 3(g) and (h), added in July 2025 (gov.il news 14.07.2025: "הקלות למשרתי מילואים בתבחינים: ספורטאי ומדריך ירי"):

- A holder under criterion 9 (shooting instructor) or 13 (athlete) whom an officer authorised by the IDF head of personnel reports as having served 30 or more reserve days cumulatively in the 90 days before the original license expired may be renewed for ONE year even without proving the criterion, provided the license was not already extended under validity regulation 4A. Proving the criterion during that year allows a renewal to 3 years counted together with that year.
- A new applicant under criterion 9 or 13 with the same reserve service in the 90 days before the application may get a one-year license without proving the criterion. An athlete applicant still needs 2 or 3 years of membership, as applicable, and 6 recognised Olympic competitions or 8 practical-shooting competitions during that period.
- For such a license, the periodic refresher falls in the second year of the combined period.

## 9. Continuity renewal after 10 years

Regulation 10(a): a person who has held a pistol lawfully for at least 10 continuous years may have the license renewed by the licensing official even if they no longer meet a criterion when applying, provided the license is valid on the day the request is filed. Regulation 10(c) limits this to licenses granted under criteria 1 to 9. Regulation 10(d) gives a similar route, for holding only, to small-bore and air rifles held for 10 continuous years before 18.10.2023.

Two consequences for the user: the route is discretionary ("rashai"), and it is lost the moment the license lapses, because a request after expiry is treated as a new application (regulation 8). A long-time holder who moved or changed jobs should renew on time.

## 10. Use restrictions

Regulation 5: a private license may not be used to provide security services as an occupation, and doing so counts as a breach of the license conditions. Carrying under criteria 13 to 16 (athlete, hunter, veterinarian, pest controller) is allowed only for the professional or sporting use for which the license was issued. Breaching a license condition is an offence under section 16(a)(2) of the Law.
