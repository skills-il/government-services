# Statutory chain, sections 170A to 170C1A, Municipalities Ordinance

## The report itself (170C(a))

Submitted to the mayor once a year, not later than **1 April** of the year following the
audited year. A copy goes to the audit committee at the same moment.

Three required contents:
1. A summary of the auditor's actions.
2. A detailing of the deficiencies found.
3. Recommendations for correcting them and preventing recurrence.

The auditor may also submit an additional report at any time, or when the mayor or the
committee demands one (170C(b)).

## The chain

| Actor | Duty | Clock | Runs from |
|---|---|---|---|
| Mayor | Comments to the committee, and a copy of the report plus comments to every council member | 3 months | Mayor receiving the report |
| Committee | Conclusions and proposals to the council for approval | 2 months | Receiving the mayor's comments |
| Council | Special discussion and decision on approval | 2 months | Committee's submission |

## The two fallback branches

**A. Mayor does not comment in time (170C(d)).** The committee discusses the report anyway
and submits its conclusions within **5 months** of the auditor's delivery to the committee.

**B. Committee does not submit, or mayor does not circulate (170C(e)(2)).** The auditor
delivers a copy of the report to every council member, and the council discusses the report
and its recommendations not later than **7 months** from submission to the mayor.

Branch B places the delivery duty on the auditor personally.

## Publication (170C(f))

No person may publish the report, a part of it, or its contents before the date set for its
submission to the council has passed, and no one may publish an audit finding. The auditor or
the mayor may permit publication only with the committee's approval.

## Evidentiary status (170C1)

Reports, opinions and any other document produced in the role are not admissible as evidence
in any legal proceeding, but are expressly not disqualified in a disciplinary proceeding.

## Scope (170A(a) to (b))

The audit covers legality, authority, purity of morals, efficiency and economy; employees'
actions; whether internal control arrangements and procedures secure compliance with every
law; and bookkeeping plus safekeeping of funds and property.

It extends to the religious council, and to any corporation, enterprise, institution, fund or
body in whose annual budget the authority participates by more than one tenth for that budget
year, or in whose management appointment it participates. Those are audited municipal bodies.

## Work plan (170A(c) to (d))

Three sources only: the auditor's discretion, the mayor's demand, or the audit committee's
demand capped at **two topics per work year**. The auditor sets the methods of carrying out
the audit at their own discretion.

## Escalations

- Suspected bribery involving the mayor or the legal advisor goes directly to the State
  Comptroller (170C(g)).
- A deficiency-correction team under section 21A(b) of the State Comptroller Law handles the
  ways and dates of correcting deficiencies after the council has dealt with the report.
