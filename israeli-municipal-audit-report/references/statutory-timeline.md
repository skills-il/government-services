# Statutory chain, sections 170A to 170C1A, Municipalities Ordinance

## The report itself (170C(a))

Submitted to the mayor once a year, not later than **1 April** of the year following the
audited year. A copy goes to the audit committee at the same moment.

Three required contents:
1. A summary of the auditor's actions.
2. A detailing of the deficiencies found.
3. Recommendations for correcting them and preventing recurrence.

The auditor may also submit an additional report at any time, or when the mayor or the
committee demands one (170C(b)).

## The chain

| Actor | Duty | Clock | Runs from |
|---|---|---|---|
| Mayor | Comments to the committee, and a copy of the report plus comments to every council member | 3 months | Mayor receiving the report |
| Committee | Conclusions and proposals to the council for approval | 2 months | Receiving the mayor's comments |
| Council | Special discussion and decision on approval | 2 months | Committee's submission |

Each clock runs from the ACTUAL date of the step before it, so a mayor who comments early
shortens the committee's deadline. Before completing its conclusions the committee may summon
officeholders of the authority or of an audited municipal body to respond (170C(d)).

## The two fallback branches

**A. Mayor does not comment in time (170C(d)).** The committee discusses the report anyway
and submits its conclusions within **5 months** of the auditor's delivery to the committee.

**B. Committee does not submit, or mayor does not circulate (170C(e)(2)).** Either trigger is
enough. Treating a mayor who missed the 3-month period as not having circulated within it is
the conservative reading. The auditor
delivers a copy of the report to every council member, and the council discusses the report
and its recommendations not later than **7 months** from submission to the mayor.

Branch B places the delivery duty on the auditor personally.

## Publication (170C(f), 334A)

Two limbs. No person may publish the report, a part of it, or its contents before the date set
for its submission to the council has passed; and no one may publish an audit finding, a limb
with no date on its face. The auditor or the mayor may permit publication only with the
committee's approval, and may attach conditions. Breach of the ban or of a permit condition is
punishable by one year's imprisonment (334A). The statute does not define the date set for
submission to the council; the conservative reading is the later of the council discussion and
the committee's statutory deadline. HCJ 7805/00 (Aloni) treats early publication by permission as
an exception for rare cases of first-rank public interest in immediate disclosure.

## Evidentiary status (170C1)

Reports, opinions and any other document produced in the role are not admissible as evidence
in any legal proceeding, but are expressly not disqualified in a disciplinary proceeding.

## Scope (170A(a) to (b))

The audit covers legality, authority, purity of morals, efficiency and economy; employees'
actions; whether internal control arrangements and procedures secure compliance with every
law; and bookkeeping plus safekeeping of funds and property.

It extends to the religious council, and to any corporation, enterprise, institution, fund or
body in whose annual budget the authority participates by more than one tenth for that budget
year, or in whose management appointment it participates. Those are audited municipal bodies.

## Work plan (170A(c) to (d))

Three sources only: the auditor's discretion, the mayor's demand, or the audit committee's
demand capped at **two topics per work year**. The auditor sets the methods of carrying out
the audit at their own discretion.

## Escalations

- Grounds to believe the mayor or the legal advisor is party to an offence under Penal Law
  Chapter E Article B go directly to the State Comptroller (170C(g)). In the current Penal Law
  that article is headed "parties to an offence"; bribery is Chapter 9 Article 5.
- A deficiency-correction team under section 21A(b) of the State Comptroller Law handles the
  ways and dates of correcting deficiencies after the council has dealt with the report
  (170C1A). Not a regional council (Local Councils Ordinance 13D).

## Deficiency-correction team clocks (170C1A(c) to (d))

| Duty | Clock | Runs from |
|---|---|---|
| Team submits recommendations to the mayor | 3 months | The council's discussion of the report |
| Team reports on implementation to the audit committee | Once every 3 months | Ongoing |
| Mayor gives written reasons to the auditor and committee for deferring a fix | Not later than 3 months | Receiving the team's recommendations |

## State Comptroller report track (separate from 170C)

| Duty | Clock | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Mayor's comments to the Comptroller | 10 weeks from receiving the report | State Comptroller Law 20(d) |
| Correction team discusses the deficiencies | 60 days from the tabling date (28(a)(1)) | 21A(c) |
| Team reports its decisions to the mayor | 15 days from the decisions | 21A(c) |
| Mayor reports the decisions to the Comptroller | 30 days from being told | 21B(a) |

Non-performance without reasonable justification is a disciplinary offence (21C). The
municipal audit committee must discuss every State Comptroller, Ombudsman and Ministry of the
Interior report on the authority and follow the correction of deficiencies (149C(a)).

## Access powers (170B)

Documents, information and explanations on demand within the period the auditor sets; access
to every database and data-processing program of the authority and audited bodies; an
invitation to every meeting of the council and its committees and of audited bodies'
committees.
