# Vehicle Ownership Transfer Guide (העברת בעלות)

## Before the Sale

### Seller Checklist
- [ ] Ensure vehicle registration (rishayon rechev) is current
- [ ] Clear any outstanding fines or fees on the vehicle
- [ ] Check the vehicle status in the government personal area for any restriction that would stop a transfer
- [ ] Gather vehicle license document and your ID
- [ ] Agree on price and payment method with buyer

### Buyer Checklist
- [ ] Check vehicle history via gov.il vehicle ownership report
- [ ] **Check for liens at the Registrar of Pledges (רשם המשכונות)** at the Ministry of Justice. This is critical and often overlooked
- [ ] Consider a pre-purchase inspection at an authorized station
- [ ] Verify the seller's identity matches the vehicle registration
- [ ] Arrange insurance (at minimum, mandatory bituach chova) to start from the transfer date
- [ ] Prepare payment and signed sale agreement

## Transfer Methods

### Online, government personal area -- 235 NIS (motorcycle 70)
**Requirements:**
- Both parties must be Israeli citizens
- Both must have gov.il accounts with digital identification
- Both must be logged in during the process
- Credit card for payment

**Process:**
1. Both parties log into gov.il
2. Seller initiates the transfer
3. Buyer confirms and pays the fee
4. Transfer is recorded immediately

### Post Office -- 257 NIS (motorcycle 87; to a licensed dealer 43)
**Requirements:**
- Both parties present in person
- Current vehicle license
- IDs of both parties (teudat zehut)
- Cash or credit card for payment

**Process:**
1. Both parties go to any Israel Post branch
2. Present documents to the clerk
3. Pay the transfer fee
4. Receive confirmation

### Licensing Bureau (Misrad Harishui)
**When to use:**
- One or both parties are not Israeli citizens
- Special circumstances (company vehicles, inheritance, etc.)

**Requirements:**
- Current vehicle license
- IDs or passports of both parties
- Power of attorney if one party cannot attend
- Additional documentation as required

## Important Rules

### How quickly must the transfer be registered?

The traffic regulations set **no express deadline** on a private seller or buyer. תקנה 284 (רישום שינוי בעלות ברכב) governs the procedure and states no time limit. A general residual clause, **תקנה 10**, supplies 15 days for any act the regulations require without stating a time ("תימסר ההודעה או תיעשה הפעולה תוך 15 יום, זולת אם יש בתקנות אלה הוראה מפורשת אחרת"). So the widely-quoted "15 days" is a construction from that default clause, not a deadline written into the transfer rules. Do not cite it to תקנה 284.

The day-figures that DO appear inside the transfer rules bind large dealers only (תקנה 284(ג), a בעל רישיון סחר averaging 500+ transfers a year): six business days to register, seven days to notify the buyer, and thirty days for the authority's confirmation. Never quote these to a private party.

What actually matters in practice:
- **Register it the same day.** There is no benefit to waiting.
- The seller remains the registered owner, and therefore carries fines, tolls and liability, until the transfer is recorded.
- Any violation in the gap is the seller's problem to unwind, not the buyer's.

### Liens and pledges (עיקולים ושעבודים)
- The government personal area (אזור אישי) does display registered liens and pledges (עיקולים ושעבודים) on the vehicle, but it is **not** the authoritative register for a pledge
- The authoritative register is the **Registrar of Pledges (רשם המשכונות)** at the Ministry of Justice. Search it separately
- A post-office counter may process a transfer even where a lien exists, so do not treat "the transfer went through" as proof the car is clean
- These are two different instruments. An עיקול is an attachment imposed in enforcement proceedings; a שעבוד / משכון is a consensual pledge registered at the Registrar of Pledges. Which one you face changes where it is registered and how it is cleared
- A registered pledge attaches to the vehicle rather than to whoever owned it at the time, which is why the register matters and why it blocks a transfer until the lender issues a release letter
- **Do not conclude that a buyer who finds one has lost the car.** A good-faith purchaser defence exists under the pledges law and whether it applies turns on the specific facts, including what was registered when and what the buyer checked. That is a question for a lawyer
- **Always check with the Registrar of Pledges before buying**

### Blocking a fraudulent transfer (חסימת העברת בעלות)

Since **27 July 2026** any private vehicle owner can log into the government personal area and **block** the ability to transfer their vehicle at an Israel Post branch. The block does not prevent the owner selling the car through the online personal-area flow, and it can be lifted at any time.

Why it exists: the Ministry identified dozens of transfers carried out by identity theft. The common scam starts with a second-hand listing. The impostor asks the seller for a **photo of the vehicle licence**, claiming they want an insurance or finance quote, then uses those details to arrange finance for a different "buyer" and register the car to them while the real owner still has the car and never sold it.

Practical rules:
- Turn the block on and leave it on while you are not selling.
- Never send a photo of your רישיון רכב or your ID to someone whose identity you have not verified.
- Sell through the personal area, where both parties authenticate through the national identification system, rather than on paper at a post office.

### When the vehicle is encumbered, leased or financed

A registered שעבוד blocks the transfer. It must be released first: the lender or leasing company issues a release letter and the pledge is deleted at the Registrar of Pledges. In an operating lease (ליסינג תפעולי) the leasing company is the owner, so the person driving the car has nothing to transfer at all. Establish who the registered owner is before agreeing a price.

### When the owner has died

A deceased owner's vehicle cannot be transferred online or at a post office. It goes through a licensing bureau with a succession order (צו ירושה) or a probate order (צו קיום צוואה). The Ministry of Transport publishes a dedicated page for this case, linked from the main ownership-transfer service.

### When the vehicle was bought with a tax exemption or discount

A vehicle purchased under a tax exemption or reduction (typically a disability-related benefit) can trigger a **repayment of the tax difference** when it is transferred. The Israel Tax Authority publishes the calculation, and it is linked from the Ministry of Transport ownership-transfer service page. Check it before pricing such a car.

### Insurance
- The buyer must arrange insurance effective from the transfer date
- At minimum, mandatory insurance (bituach chova) is required
- Driving without valid insurance is a criminal offense in Israel
- The seller's insurance does not automatically transfer to the buyer
